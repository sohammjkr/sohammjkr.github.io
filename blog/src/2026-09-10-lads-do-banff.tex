\title{The Lads do Banff}
\date{2026-09-10 to 2026-09-14}
\location{Banff, AB - Calgary, AB}
\tags{travel, friends, food}

\begin{document}

\lead{Turquoise lakes on repeat, a glacier underfoot, and four men arguing
about bed assignments minutes after meeting for the first time in months.}

The four of us converged on Calgary within about two hours of each other and
rented a car. While we waited on Choidee's flight to
land we grabbed shawarma from Jerusalem Shawarma, a Calgary chain, and then
went straight to mini golf, because every Lads trip starts with a round of
mini golf and the scores decide room assignments. We play under monikers, not
names, which is its own tradition and explains the table below.

\begin{tabular}{lccc}
  Player & Front 9 & Back 9 & Total \\
  Choidee & 29 & 28 & 57 \\
  Shwarma & 34 & 25 & 59 \\
  Choccy Milk & 33 & 27 & 60 \\
  Green Curry & 33 & 34 & 67 \\
\end{tabular}

I am not going to expose who is who. What I will say is that I am Green
Curry, I got last, and I got the sofabed. Some things you just have to sit
with.

From there it was straight to Banff, in the door at Tunnel Mountain Resort
just as check-in opened, and out again immediately to walk downtown before
dinner at The Boss.

\img[70]{/assets/BanffBowRiverBridge.jpg}{Crossing the Bow River, first
evening, still arguing about something.}

\img{/assets/BanffTheBossDinner.jpg}{Dinner at The Boss. Nobody's plate
survived the photo.}

\sep

Lake Louise started absurdly early and only got better from there.

\img{/assets/BanffLakeLouiseSunrise.jpg}{Lake Louise, stupid early, dead
calm.}

We hiked up to Lake Agnes and ate breakfast at the teahouse, which is exactly
as good an idea as it sounds when you have just climbed a mountain for it.

\img[70]{/assets/BanffTeahouseSnacks.jpg}{Hot chocolate and a baked good at
the Lake Agnes Teahouse. The tuna sandwich did not survive.}

We rounded out the morning at Lake Moraine before the crowds caught up to
us. Dinner that night was at Zyka, the Indian place in Banff, which four
Indian guys agreeing to eat Indian food on a trip to the Canadian Rockies
should tell you everything about our priorities.

\img{/assets/BanffZykaDinner.jpg}{Dinner at Zyka. The table did not stay
this tidy for long.}

\sep

The Icefields Parkway is the kind of drive where you stop every fifteen
minutes and mean it every time. Peyto Lake alone is worth the whole road trip.

\img[75]{/assets/BanffPeytoLake.jpg}{Peyto Lake. The color is not a filter.}

The turnaround point was the Columbia Icefield, where we strapped on crampons
and walked out onto the Athabasca Glacier, which is a genuinely strange
sensation, ice under your boots and a sheer wall of rock on either side.

\img[80]{/assets/BanffGlacierWalk.jpg}{On the Athabasca Glacier. Everyone
smiling, nobody warm.}

We closed the loop back in Banff for dinner at Pizzeria Sophia, which felt
earned after a day spent mostly outdoors in the cold.

\sep

Our last full day was the greatest hits tour: Lake Minnewanka and Two Jack
Lake in the morning, the Banff Gondola up Sulphur Mountain for lunch at Sky
Bistro, and Johnston Canyon on the way out of town. Two Jack Lake in
particular was dead calm and fogged over, which made for the best kind of
morning, quiet, cold, and completely worth getting up for.

\img[70]{/assets/BanffTwoJackLake.jpg}{Two Jack Lake, holding still for once.}

We closed out the trip in Calgary with dinner at Khao San Thai before an early
flight home the next morning. Five days, four lads, one mini golf loss I still
maintain was rigged.
