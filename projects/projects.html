Regenerative Clamp Converter

MS thesis · Center for Distributed Energy, Georgia Tech · Advisor: Dr. Deepak Divan
2025MS Thesis·power electronicsthesishardwaremagneticssolar #
Read the thesis (PDF)

Design and analysis of regenerative clamp converters that recover leakage-inductor energy in high-power isolated converter topologies, reducing device stress and improving efficiency. The clamp architecture was then extended into a multiport interface, so the auxiliary port can integrate PV or another distributed energy resource while still doing its job as a clamp.

The problem

In high-power isolated topologies, energy stored in the transformer’s leakage inductance has to go somewhere at every switching transition. A dissipative clamp turns it into heat and voltage stress on the devices. Recovering that energy instead of burning it buys back efficiency and lets the switches be sized against a lower peak voltage.

What the work covers

  • Analysis and modelling of the regenerative clamp, including the conditions under which leakage energy is returned to the source rather than dissipated.
  • Reduction of device voltage stress and the resulting effect on converter efficiency in high-power isolated topologies.
  • Extension of the clamp into a multiport interface: the auxiliary port carries a PV or DER input while the clamping function is preserved.
  • Simulation and hardware validation of the proposed architecture.

Medium-Voltage Inertia-Less Isolated Converter

Center for Distributed Energy, Georgia Tech · Atlanta, GA
2024 – 2025Prototyped·power electronicshardwaremagneticscontrolsgrid #

Design and prototyping of a multi-level, medium-voltage, single-stage isolated converter — 800 Vdc to 2.4 kVac per slice. The single-stage approach removes the intermediate DC link that a conventional two-stage design needs, which takes out a large electrolytic capacitor bank and the reliability problem that comes with it.

Scope of the work

  • Topology definition for the multi-level medium-voltage stage.
  • High-frequency magnetics design, including the isolation transformer.
  • Control and protection implementation.
  • Hardware bring-up, firmware, and test and validation on the bench.

Integrated Home Energy Fast Charger

Center for Distributed Energy, Georgia Tech · Atlanta, GA
2024 – 2025Patent filed·power electronicsev chargingsolarstoragehardware #
Patent disclosure

A 50 kW bidirectional home fast charger that takes 120 Vac split-phase in and delivers 250–400 Vdc to a vehicle, with PV and storage folded into the same box rather than bolted on beside it.

Architecture

  • Interleaved PFC front end on the split-phase input.
  • Active power decoupling, which removes the bulk energy-storage capacitance a single-phase system would otherwise need.
  • Isolated DC–DC stage built on SiC MOSFETs.
  • Bidirectional operation, so the vehicle can support the house as well as draw from it.
  • Automatic integration of PV and storage alongside EV charging.

AC Cube — Single-Stage PV/Battery/Grid Energy Router

Center for Distributed Energy, Georgia Tech · ECCE-Asia 2025, Bengaluru
2024 – 2025Published·power electronicspublicationgridsolarstoragecontrols #
Paper (IEEE Xplore)

A single-stage isolated converter that unifies PV, battery and grid into one conversion stage instead of the usual stack of separate converters. Built on the inertia-less isolated converter, the prototype delivers 240 Vac from a 48 Vdc source using a \(\Sigma\)–\(\Delta\) modulation scheme.

Why single-stage

A conventional PV-plus-storage installation chains several converters together, each with its own DC link, its own capacitor bank and its own losses. Collapsing that into one isolated stage removes the intermediate storage element — the “inertia” — and with it the component that most often ends the system’s life.

Approach

  • Bidirectional single-stage isolated topology with an ultra-low-leakage transformer.
  • Pulse density modulation (\(\Sigma\)–\(\Delta\)) for AC synthesis.
  • Zero-voltage and zero-current switching across the operating range.
  • AC-side switching, with PV, battery pack and grid all routed through the one stage.

Solar Plug — Universal Off-Grid Microconverter

Center for Distributed Energy, Georgia Tech · IDEAS 2025
2024 – 2025Published·power electronicspublicationsolarhardware #
Paper (IEEE Xplore)

A plug-and-play microconverter for off-grid PV: an isolated inline converter with onboard MPPT, aimed at Tier-1 through Tier-4 energy access at a cost that makes sense in the markets that need it.

The idea

Energy access at the low end fails on cost and installation complexity long before it fails on physics. Solar Plug puts the conversion and the maximum power point tracking inline in a single small unit, so a panel and a load can be connected without a designed system around them.

  • Isolated inline conversion with onboard MPPT.
  • Direct PV inversion, targeting decentralised and off-grid deployment.
  • Cost and power density traded explicitly against Tier-1–4 access requirements.
  • Simulation and hardware prototypes.

Universal Minimal Converter — Multiport HF-Link DC/DC/AC

Center for Distributed Energy, Georgia Tech · ECCE 2025
2025Published·power electronicspublicationsolarstoragegrid #
Paper (IEEE Xplore)

A multiport bidirectional high-frequency-link split-phase DC/DC/AC converter: one high-frequency isolation stage serving several ports, so PV, storage and a split-phase AC output share the same magnetics instead of each needing their own converter.

Approach

  • Tri-port architecture around a single high-frequency link.
  • Bidirectional DC/AC conversion, single-stage.
  • Ultra-low leakage inductance transformer design.
  • Sigma-delta modulation for AC synthesis.
  • Split-phase output, matching North American residential service.

Rogowski-Coil Analog Current Sensor

Center for Distributed Energy, Georgia Tech · Atlanta, GA
2024 – 2025Validated·power electronicshardwarecontrolsmeasurement #

A wideband, low-cost analog Rogowski coil current sensor, designed for closed-loop current control in high-frequency power converters.

Why build one

Commercial current sensors with the bandwidth these converters need are expensive and physically large, which is a problem when the whole point of the converter is density. A Rogowski coil is inherently non-intrusive and cheap; the difficulty is the integrator that turns \(\mathrm{d}i/\mathrm{d}t\) back into a usable current signal without drift or bandwidth loss.

The work

  • Analog integrator design on PCB, with parameter sweeps across the integrator network.
  • Bandwidth and accuracy characterisation against a reference sensor.
  • Validation in closed-loop current control of a high-frequency converter.

Machine Learning for Weld Acoustics Monitoring

Caterpillar Inc. and University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign · Peoria, IL
2023 – 2024Delivered·machine learningembeddeduiuc #
Final report (DOCX)

A system that classifies weld quality from the sound of the weld itself. Welding produces a characteristic acoustic signature; the question was whether that signature carries enough information to flag a bad weld as it happens, without instrumenting the workpiece.

Pipeline

  • MFCC feature extraction from raw acoustic recordings, in Python with Librosa.
  • SVM classifiers trained on Caterpillar welding lab datasets.
  • Python GUI giving the operator immediate feedback.
  • Arduino and LED prototype alert stack for a shop-floor signal.

Result

75–84% test accuracy on the Caterpillar welding lab datasets.
