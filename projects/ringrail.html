RingRail: A Self-Identifying, Self-Synchronizing Ring-Bus Communication Protocol for FPGAs in Medium-Voltage Applications

GridTran · Atlanta, GA
2026·ControlPatent

RingRail is the fibre control bus for a modular medium-voltage converter. One main FPGA commands a series stack of bridge boards, each with its own FPGA floating at a different potential, over optical rings. A board learns its address from where it sits in the ring, and every board switches on the same edge however far down the ring it is.

How it works

  • Two trains per ring. A slow train carries CRC-checked command frames out and status back. A fast train carries only a timing tick. The slow train says what to do, the fast train says when.
  • Self-identifying. A board’s index is its position in the ring, picked up from the traffic. There is no address switch, every board of a class runs the same image, and two boards can be swapped without reconfiguring anything.
  • Self-synchronizing. Each board delays its trigger by the hops still ahead of the pulse, so all of them fire together, and a local phase-locked loop tracks the main FPGA across crystal offsets and rides over a lost tick.
  • Fail-safe. The main FPGA watches its own frames come back around the ring. A broken ring or a lost timebase takes every board to a safe state.

Patent

D. Divan, J. Benzaquen, P. R. Nallamatti, S. Manjrekar and S. Itum, “Self-Identifying, Self-Synchronizing Ring-Bus Communication Protocol for FPGAs in Medium-Voltage Applications,” provisional patent disclosure, GridTran, April 2026.
