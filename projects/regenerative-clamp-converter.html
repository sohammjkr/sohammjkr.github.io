Regenerative Clamp Converter

MS thesis · Center for Distributed Energy, Georgia Tech · Advisor: Dr. Deepak Divan
2025MS Thesis·ConvertersGT

The Regenerative Clamp Converter (RCC) is an energy capture and recovery circuit for the Inertia-less Isolated Converter (IIC), a single-stage, high-frequency isolated converter with no DC link. Every switch transition traps energy in the transformer’s leakage inductance. A conventional clamp burns it as heat; the RCC captures it and returns it to the main power path through a small series recovery transformer. It sits wholly on one side of the isolation barrier, so the host converter’s modulation and primary-side bridge are unchanged.

The problem

The IIC is an isolated DC/AC matrix converter. It has no intermediate energy storage, so the energy trapped in the leakage inductance and the device parasitics has nowhere to go. The energy is released at each switch transition and drives the secondary voltage toward twice the reflected DC voltage. A clamp is required to drive this converter to higher power levels, and while dissipative clamps exist, an active clamp may enable higher converter efficiencies.
